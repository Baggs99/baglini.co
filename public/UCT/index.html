Challenge 1: leadership succession

Priority 1

What we heard

  • Production sits with one senior co-founder, with no named successor
  • No contingency plan if the production lead is unavailable
  • Four owners, split 38 / 26 / 26 / 10
  • Roles are informal; the business runs on unpaid owner effort
  • Leadership conversation set for next month

The risk

  • Losing or sidelining the production lead halts the wine and the business
  • Decisions rest on the same few people
  • Customer and funder relationships live in people's heads, not in a system
  • No agreed buy-out or succession terms among owners

The gap on contingency planning came up in our group conversation with the client. It is not in the written thread.

The recommended leadership team

CEO

  • Goal: quality and impact balanced on one scorecard
  • Owns the shareholder agreement and succession plan
  • Overlaps with the CRO on funders and CSI
  • Overlaps with Operations on production sign-off

Chief Relationship Officer

  • Goal: grow customers, funders, school partners and collaborations
  • Owns the CRM and the Founders' Club
  • Leads the school partnership relationship
  • Overlaps with Operations on order fulfilment

Operations Officer

  • Goal: a documented, reliable production and supply chain
  • Owns vineyard, supply, production and e-commerce fulfilment
  • Tracks school permits and compliance steps
  • Overlaps with the CRO on e-commerce orders

CRM

One shared record of customers, funders, school partners and orders. Nothing lives in one person's phone.

Fortnightly meeting

A 60-minute meeting every two weeks. Each member reports on goals and open actions.

Cross-role proficiency

Every role has a named backup, trained to cover its core tasks.

Challenge 2: the school vineyard approval path

1

SGB and principal

Minute approval for use of land and water, and that learners maintain the vineyard. Without this, WCED will not engage.

2

WCED district office

Schools are provincial land. Infrastructure and Facilities approve digging deeper than 500 mm, trellising, or changes to water use.

3

Other departments

Client lists Basic Education, Agriculture and Public Works. Trade, Industry and Competition may also be needed.

4

Outcome

One application denied. The other two have progressed at an unequal rate, which points to gaps in the process.

The denied application is the clearest sign of fragmented government process. The land was reserved for future school development.

Our recommendation

Lead with leadership. A CEO, a Chief Relationship Officer and an Operations Officer, with overlapping goals and named backups, run from a shared CRM and a fortnightly meeting.

  • 1Succession: name a successor for production and document the know-how
  • 2Roles: define the three roles, their goals, and the overlaps between them
  • 3CRM: one shared record of customers, funders, school partners and orders
  • 4Rhythm: a fortnightly leadership meeting so every member knows where things stand
  • 5School path: concentrate on the furthest-along school and start a phased first step

3–6 months

  • Appoint the first paid role

  • CRM in daily use across all roles
  • Draft the shareholder agreement

6–12 months

  • Shareholder agreement signed
  • Submit a complete application for the furthest school
  • Scorecard live and reported
  • Succession review

Steps in the first column need the owners' decision at next month's leadership meeting.

Measuring progress and managing risk

Scorecard

  • Quality: competition medals, sensory scores, repeat purchase
  • Impact: learners engaged, bursaries, jobs, township-sourced fruit
  • Business: revenue mix across channels and funders
  • Reviewed at each fortnightly meeting

Risks and mitigations

  • Key-person dependence → succession plan, documented know-how, named backups
  • School approvals delayed or denied → triage schools, phased first step
  • Quality slips under impact pressure → one scorecard covering both
  • Reliance on one donor or channel → diversify funding and sales
